10 Hidden Reasons Shopify Brands Struggle to Increase AOV

If your store gets traffic and orders but customers are not adding more products, the problem is rarely the traffic. It is usually one of these ten things.

← Back to blog
Aug 8, 20269 min readAOV diagnostics

Shopify brands often spend time and money increasing website traffic, improving advertisements, and attracting new customers. However, many stores still receive small orders containing only one product.

This makes growth more difficult because customer acquisition costs continue to affect profit.

Increasing average order value does not mean forcing customers to spend more. It means helping them discover useful products, bundles, upgrades, and rewards that improve their original purchase.

If your store receives traffic and orders but customers are not adding more products, here are 10 hidden reasons Shopify brands struggle to increase AOV.

1. Why Are Customers Buying Only One Product?

Many Shopify stores are built around individual products rather than complete customer needs.

For example, a skincare store may promote only a face wash instead of presenting a complete routine. A fitness store may sell protein powder without showing a shaker, snack, or supplement that works with it.

When customers do not see a clear connection between products, they purchase only the item they originally wanted.

The solution is to group products based on customer needs. Instead of asking, “What else can we sell?” ask, “What else will help the customer get a better result?”

Examples include:

  • Face wash with moisturizer
  • Coffee beans with a filter
  • Protein powder with a shaker
  • Dress with a matching accessory
  • Cloth diaper with an extra insert

Relevant recommendations can increase order value while also making the purchase more useful. Shopify describes cross-selling as recommending complementary products that complete the original purchase.

2. Why Are Shopify Cart Upsells Not Converting?

A common cart upsell Shopify mistake is showing the same recommendation to every customer.

A person buying baby clothing should not see an unrelated adult fashion product. A customer buying an entry-level item may also ignore an expensive add-on that doubles the cart total.

Cart upsells work best when they are:

  • Relevant to the selected product
  • Lower-priced than the main purchase
  • Easy to understand
  • Simple to add with one click
  • Clearly connected to a customer benefit

Shopify recommends hand-picking products that naturally match items in the cart rather than showing general popular products. It also suggests keeping the price of an additional item reasonable compared with the original purchase.

3. Are You Showing Too Many Offers in the Cart?

More offers do not always create more revenue.

Some carts contain a coupon field, countdown timer, free gift, multiple upsells, product recommendations, shipping messages, and several announcement banners.

This can make the cart feel crowded and difficult to understand. Customers may stop reviewing the offers and move directly to checkout or leave because the experience feels confusing.

A better approach is to prioritize the offers. For example:

  1. Show one relevant product recommendation.
  2. Display one clear reward target.
  3. Keep the checkout button easy to find.

A simple cart with one strong recommendation is often more helpful than several unrelated promotions.

4. Are Your Product Bundles Solving a Real Need?

Creating a bundle does not automatically increase AOV.

Some brands combine products only because they want to clear inventory. However, customers are more likely to purchase a bundle when the products create a clear solution.

Useful bundle ideas include:

  • Complete skincare routine
  • Monthly snack pack
  • New-parent essentials
  • Beginner fitness kit
  • Full outfit combination
  • Travel care kit

Explain why the products belong together. Show the individual product value, total bundle price, savings, and result the customer can expect.

Shopify states that product bundles can increase average order value while giving customers value through product curation and discounts.

5. Is Your Free-Shipping Threshold Too High?

Free shipping can motivate customers to add another product, but only when the target feels achievable.

Suppose your current AOV is $40. A free-shipping target of $50 or $55 may encourage customers to add a small product. A target of $100 may feel too far away and could be ignored.

Display the remaining amount clearly:

You are $8 away from free shipping.

Then recommend products priced close to that amount.

Shopify allows merchants to offer free shipping automatically when an order reaches a set minimum value. Its current guidance also identifies free-shipping minimums as an AOV strategy.

6. Are Discounts Reducing Profit Instead of Improving AOV?

Discounts can increase the amount customers spend, but they can also reduce profit.

For example, a customer may add another product to receive a 20% storewide discount. The order value increases, but the business may earn less after product costs, shipping, payment charges, and advertising expenses.

A strong average order value strategy should measure both revenue and margin.

Instead of giving every customer a large discount, test offers such as:

  • Buy two and save 10%
  • Spend $75 and receive a gift
  • Add a related product at a special price
  • Buy a larger pack and save per unit
  • Complete a bundle for a smaller combined saving

The goal is to encourage a larger purchase without removing too much profit.

7. Is Your Cart Designed Only for Checkout?

Many Shopify carts show only the product name, quantity, price, subtotal, and checkout button.

This makes checkout simple, but it misses an important product-discovery opportunity.

A cart can also show:

  • Relevant product upsells
  • Frequently bought together products
  • Free-shipping progress
  • Available rewards
  • Bundle savings
  • Coupon offers
  • Product add-ons

The cart should help customers review their order and discover anything useful they may have missed.

However, cart optimization should not block or delay checkout. Recommendations must support the buying experience rather than interrupt it.

8. Are You Using the Same AOV Strategy for Every Customer?

Not every customer should receive the same offer.

A new customer may respond well to a trial-size product. A repeat customer may prefer a larger pack or subscription. A high-value customer may be interested in a premium bundle.

Recommendations can be adjusted using information such as:

  • Product being viewed
  • Products in the cart
  • Current cart value
  • Previous orders
  • New or returning customer
  • Product category
  • Buying frequency

Shopify includes AI-powered product recommendations as one method for improving AOV because recommendations can be based on customer behaviour and product relationships.

Personalization does not need to be complicated. Start by connecting each bestseller with two or three products that naturally complement it.

9. Are You Measuring AOV Without Checking Conversion Rate?

AOV should not be measured alone.

Imagine your AOV increases from $50 to $65, but your conversion rate falls because the cart becomes confusing. The store may receive larger orders but fewer completed purchases.

Track AOV together with:

  • Conversion rate
  • Revenue per visitor
  • Cart abandonment rate
  • Upsell acceptance rate
  • Bundle revenue
  • Gross profit per order
  • Refund rate
  • Returning customer rate

Shopify recommends using a focused group of metrics, including conversion rate, average order value, and customer acquisition cost, when evaluating ecommerce performance.

A successful AOV optimization strategy should increase order value without creating major checkout friction.

10. Are You Testing Too Many AOV Tactics at Once?

Some Shopify brands activate bundles, cart upsells, free gifts, quantity discounts, coupons, and post-purchase offers at the same time.

When AOV changes, they do not know which offer created the result.

Use a step-by-step testing process:

  1. Record your current AOV and conversion rate.
  2. Choose one bestselling product.
  3. Add one relevant cart upsell.
  4. Run the test for a suitable period.
  5. Measure clicks, acceptance, revenue, and profit.
  6. Keep, improve, or remove the offer.
  7. Test the next opportunity.

How Can Shopify Brands Build a Better Average Order Value Strategy?

A strong average order value strategy begins with understanding what customers need after choosing their first product.

Start with these questions:

  • Which products are frequently purchased together?
  • What small product completes the main purchase?
  • What cart value is close to the current AOV?
  • Which bundle solves a complete customer problem?
  • Which offer produces additional profit?
  • Where are customers leaving the buying journey?

Do not begin by adding more discounts. Begin by improving product relevance and the cart experience.

Shopify brands struggle to increase AOV when they treat it as a single discount campaign. AOV optimization connects merchandising, pricing, bundles, cart design, customer behaviour, and measurement.

The most effective strategy is usually simple: show the right product, to the right customer, at the right stage of the buying journey.
